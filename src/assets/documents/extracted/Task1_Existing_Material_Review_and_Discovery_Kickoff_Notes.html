2. Current Product Understanding

RetirementTrack is currently envisioned as a multi-tenant retirement education, assessment and recurring-engagement platform for professional firms and their clients. The product is intended to combine planning status, assessments, educational resources, reminders, benchmarking and advisor follow-up into a coherent experience rather than operate as a collection of disconnected forms or content. The additional UX guidance strengthens this direction by stating that the client experience should feel personalized and evolving around the individual client, not like a generic library of retirement resources.

The central product hypothesis is that the platform creates relevant reasons for clients to return, while giving advisors a scalable way to understand what has changed, what needs attention, and where human follow-up is valuable. The first credible MVP should therefore be intentionally focused but polished enough to demonstrate the actual RetirementTrack proposition: it should know enough about the client's stage, circumstances, assessments and history to surface relevant priorities, content and next actions without requiring sophisticated AI.

Additional UX guidance impact: Personalization architecture and the first-login/client-return experience are now explicit MVP considerations. Assessment responses, client attributes, life stage, content tags, completion history and firm controls may all contribute to what is prioritized and displayed to the client.

Current Three-Layer Product Model

LayerCurrent understanding
Client ExperiencePersonalized dashboard showing client name/context, retirement stage, planning status/progress, completed and outstanding items, a small set of prioritized items that deserve attention, opportunities, reminders, assessments, Planning Updates, dynamically relevant educational content, benchmarking, and relevant factoids. The experience should reinforce continuity: what was completed, what changed and what comes next.
Advisor ExperienceDashboard across clients showing invitations/communications, assigned and completed assessments, Planning Updates due/completed/overdue, changes, alerts, follow-up needs, tasks and communication history. Advisor interaction should remain visible in the client experience so RetirementTrack supports, rather than replaces, the professional relationship.
Firm ControlFirm-level control over features, client visibility, communications, content, templates, branding and advisor assignment, with communication modes such as Automatic, Advisor Approval and Off.

Recurring Engagement Model

Client data / life stage / assessments / circumstances → rules & personalization → prioritized client experience → relevant content & actions → advisor interaction → updated client state → next relevant reason to return

7. Current Assumptions Requiring Validation

IDAssumption
A-01The MVP serves professional firms/advisors and their clients rather than a direct-to-consumer model.
A-02The client dashboard is the primary client entry point and must consolidate status, actions, opportunities, education and reminders.
A-03Planning Updates are a core MVP workflow and an important recurring-engagement mechanism.
A-04Firms retain control over which features and communications are enabled for their clients.
A-05Some controls may also be applied at the individual client level.
A-06Communications may support Automatic, Advisor Approval and Off modes.
A-07MVP personalization can begin with explicit rules using age/life stage, client attributes, assessment results, Planning Update state, advisor assignments and content tags; sophisticated AI is not required to demonstrate the concept.
A-08Benchmarking is important to the proposed value proposition but its methodology is not yet finalized.
A-09Basic white-label branding and assigned-advisor identity are sufficient for the first release unless Discovery identifies a stronger requirement.
A-10Advanced integrations, analytics, AI and highly configurable themes can be deferred unless required to validate the core product.
A-11Personalization architecture and first-login/client-return behavior are part of the MVP baseline and should not be postponed entirely to a later phase.
A-12The MVP should present a concise prioritized experience (for example, a small number of items deserving attention) rather than a long generic list of resources/tasks.
A-13Assessment responses and client history/state are expected to influence what is surfaced to the client, subject to business rules defined during Discovery.

9. Initial Risks / Concerns

IDRisk / concernWhy it mattersInitial Discovery response
R-01MVP scope expansionThe current vision contains many attractive capabilities; collectively they can become a large first release.Prioritize the core engagement loop and explicitly simplify/defer secondary depth during Discovery.
R-02Assessment/scoring ambiguityAssessment questions, scoring, categories and outputs are not yet defined.Identify content/methodology owner and define the minimum assessment model before technical design is finalized.
R-03Planning Update complexityThis core workflow could expand into configurable forms, workflow automation and task management.Define a narrow MVP state model, fixed initial form/rules and specific follow-up behavior.
R-04Benchmark methodologyBenchmarking can be differentiated value but depends on credible source data, cohort definitions and privacy/caveats.Validate source/methodology early and consider a curated/reference-data approach if live cohort analytics is not MVP-ready.
R-05Communication complexityAutomatic/approval/off plus multiple triggers/channels/templates can become a notification platform.Start with limited channels/triggers and a clear approval model; preserve extensibility.
R-06Privacy / client dataPersonalization, assessments and financial benchmarks may require sensitive client data.Use data minimization; identify required fields, retention, access, logging and deletion rules during Discovery.
R-07Tenant configuration complexityFirm and client-level toggles can create inheritance/override and support complexity.Define a small configuration hierarchy with clear defaults and auditability.
R-08Advisor dashboard overloadTrying to surface every alert/task/client metric can make the first advisor experience noisy.Prioritize actionable exceptions and follow-up states over broad analytics.
R-09White-label scope growthCustom domains, deep themes and branded messaging can materially increase implementation/testing.Confirm a minimal white-label package for MVP.
R-10Future integration pressureCRM/email/calendar/planning-system integrations may distract from validating the core product.Design stable integration seams, but defer integrations unless required to operate the MVP.
R-11Personalization scope expansionPersonalization can quickly expand into recommendation engines, AI, segmentation and complex ranking logic.Constrain MVP to explicit, understandable rules and a limited set of inputs/outputs that demonstrate the client-specific experience.
R-12Rules / prioritization complexityIndependent rules across assessments, content, opportunities and reminders could become inconsistent and difficult to maintain.Define one coherent MVP relevance/prioritization model with clear precedence and ownership before implementation.
R-13Insufficient client contextA personalized first-login experience may be weak when the platform has limited client data.Define minimum onboarding/profile inputs, sensible empty/default states and progressive personalization as more information becomes available.
R-14Advisor trust and controlSystem-generated priorities or content may conflict with an advisor's judgment or firm preferences.Define advisor/firm visibility, suppression/override and approval controls for the MVP where needed.
