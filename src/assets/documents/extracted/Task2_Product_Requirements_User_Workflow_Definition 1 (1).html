RETIREMENTTRACK

Product Requirements &User Workflow Definition

1. Purpose and Scope

Goal: Translate the product vision into buildable, testable user behavior for the first credible MVP without prematurely designing the technical architecture.

Product vision: A white-label retirement education, assessment, and ongoing-engagement platform for professional firms and clients approaching or living in retirement.

MVP principle: The MVP should not feel like a generic library of forms and retirement resources. The client experience should use known client context, life stage, assessments, circumstances, completion history, and firm/advisor inputs to present a small number of relevant next actions, content, and planning opportunities.

1.1 What the product is intended to prove

• Clients can understand where they are, what is complete, what needs attention, and what is relevant next.

• Assessments, Planning Updates, education, reminders, and selected benchmarking can participate in one coherent client experience.

• Advisors can see activity, exceptions, changes, and follow-up needs without manually reviewing every client.

• Recurring engagement creates meaningful reasons for clients and advisors to return over time.

3. Client Experience

Client outcome: The client should feel that RetirementTrack knows enough about their stage and activity to present a purposeful next step, not a generic library of retirement resources.

First-Build Client ExperienceLater / Expanded
Personalized opening; simple planning status; completed/outstanding work; a small attention list; assessments; Planning Updates; relevant education; reminders/milestones; advisor identity; continuity between visits.Sophisticated AI recommendations; large dynamic content journeys; complex retirement-readiness scoring; highly configurable client journeys.

4. Client Onboarding Flow

4.1 First-Build behavior

• Collect only the minimum profile/context required to make the first dashboard useful.

• Establish the correct firm and advisor relationship before the client reaches the dashboard.

• Show a useful starting state even when personalization data is limited.

• Retain basic invitation, activation, consent and onboarding status/history.

• Avoid unsupported benchmark or planning conclusions when required inputs are missing.

4.2 Product decisions to confirm

• Exact invitation expiration/resend policy.

• Exact terms/privacy/communication-consent requirements.

• Whether Firm Administrator and Advisor both create/invite clients.

• Minimum age/life-stage fields required at activation.

• Behavior for existing accounts, duplicate clients, multiple firms/advisors.

6. Advisor Experience

• Focus the first build on actionability rather than broad analytics.

• Keep professional judgment with the advisor; RetirementTrack surfaces conditions and context rather than individualized advice.

• Allow assignment/recommendation only for product capabilities enabled by the firm.

• Provide enough history to understand what was sent, completed, changed and followed up.

9. Platform Administrator Experience

• Manage tenant/firms, tenant status, common content and supported platform defaults.

• Support operational troubleshooting without becoming part of ordinary advisor-client workflows.

• Keep direct access to sensitive client details restricted by default.

• Maintain basic administrative/audit visibility required for tenant operations.

10. Assessment Workflow

First BuildNot Assumed / Later
Small number of structured assessments; risk assessment is a likely example; readable results; defined scoring/category logic; educational context; advisor follow-up cue.General-purpose assessment builder; dozens of assessments; firm-customizable forms/workflows; broad report engine.

11. Planning Update Workflow

11.1 First-Build intent

• Treat Planning Update as a core recurring-engagement workflow.

• Use a deliberately narrow initial form/state model rather than a configurable workflow engine.

• Compare current information with relevant prior state to identify defined changes.

• Only create advisor attention when business rules identify a meaningful change.

• Update client state/history after completion.

12. Recurring Engagement Workflow

First-build triggers may come from: Planning Update due/overdue, assessment assignment/completion, advisor assignment, age/life-stage milestone, newly relevant content, or a defined change/opportunity. Exact trigger inventory is still a Product Decision.

Automate in First BuildKeep Advisor-Controlled
Due/status calculations; basic reminder scheduling; completion/state tracking; simple personalization rules; defined change/follow-up creation; communication history.Professional interpretation; individualized advice; complex recommendations; arbitrary campaign creation; custom workflow/rule authoring.

13. Communication Workflow

ModeBehavior
AutomaticSend when the defined condition occurs.
Advisor ApprovalPrepare/queue; advisor reviews before send.

Likely first-build communication use cases: Onboarding invitation, Planning Update invitation/reminder, assessment assignment/reminder, selected milestone/general planning reminders where defined.

14. Advisor Review / Follow-Up Workflow

First-build principle: A simple review lifecycle is enough. RetirementTrack should surface why attention is needed without turning into a full CRM/task-management platform.

Potential TriggerFirst-Build Treatment
Meaningful Planning Update changeCreate advisor review item when rule is defined.
Assessment result conditionCreate review cue when methodology/business rule requires it.
Pending communication approvalShow in advisor work queue.
Overdue/incomplete client itemOnly create follow-up if the business rule says advisor attention is warranted.
Routine completionRecord as activity; do not automatically create noise.

15. Educational Content Delivery Workflow

First-build content: Platform-provided articles, videos, resources and other approved educational items, with enough tagging to support simple relevance and firm visibility controls.

Later-phase capability
Firm-specific authoring/uploads, sophisticated recommendation engines, complex campaign journeys and large content-personalization taxonomies should not be assumed for the first build.

16. Reminder Workflow

16.1 First-Build reminder scope

• Basic age/date/event reminders as described in the Discovery Brief.

• Planning Update and assessment reminders where selected for MVP.

• Noise controls: no reminder after completion, avoid duplicates, limit repeated reminders.

17. Benchmarking Workflow

MVP position: Benchmarking remains part of the stated product vision. However, the first build should only include a simplified benchmark experience if credible measures, data source, cohort methodology, privacy rules and caveats are validated during Discovery.

Do not assume: Live cohort analytics, complex historical benchmarking, sophisticated readiness scoring, or benchmark-driven financial recommendations.

18. Personalization Workflow

Belongs in First BuildLater / Avoid Overbuilding
Personalized opening; age/life-stage relevance; assessment-driven relevance where rules exist; small “what deserves attention” list; dynamic education; progress/continuity; advisor connection.AI/ML recommendation engine; complex segmentation; firm-authored rule builder; large configurable decision engine.
