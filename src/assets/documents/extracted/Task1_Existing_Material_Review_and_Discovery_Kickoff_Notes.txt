# 2. Current Product Understanding
RetirementTrack is currently envisioned as a multi-tenant retirement education, assessment and recurring-engagement platform for professional firms and their clients. The product is intended to combine planning status, assessments, educational resources, reminders, benchmarking and advisor follow-up into a coherent experience rather than operate as a collection of disconnected forms or content. The additional UX guidance strengthens this direction by stating that the client experience should feel personalized and evolving around the individual client, not like a generic library of retirement resources.
The central product hypothesis is that the platform creates relevant reasons for clients to return, while giving advisors a scalable way to understand what has changed, what needs attention, and where human follow-up is valuable. The first credible MVP should therefore be intentionally focused but polished enough to demonstrate the actual RetirementTrack proposition: it should know enough about the client's stage, circumstances, assessments and history to surface relevant priorities, content and next actions without requiring sophisticated AI.
Additional UX guidance impact: Personalization architecture and the first-login/client-return experience are now explicit MVP considerations. Assessment responses, client attributes, life stage, content tags, completion history and firm controls may all contribute to what is prioritized and displayed to the client.
## Current Three-Layer Product Model
Layer
Current understanding
Client Experience
Personalized dashboard showing client name/context, retirement stage, planning status/progress, completed and outstanding items, a small set of prioritized items that deserve attention, opportunities, reminders, assessments, Planning Updates, dynamically relevant educational content, benchmarking, and relevant factoids. The experience should reinforce continuity: what was completed, what changed and what comes next.
Advisor Experience
Dashboard across clients showing invitations/communications, assigned and completed assessments, Planning Updates due/completed/overdue, changes, alerts, follow-up needs, tasks and communication history. Advisor interaction should remain visible in the client experience so RetirementTrack supports, rather than replaces, the professional relationship.
Firm Control
Firm-level control over features, client visibility, communications, content, templates, branding and advisor assignment, with communication modes such as Automatic, Advisor Approval and Off.
## Recurring Engagement Model
Client data / life stage / assessments / circumstances → rules & personalization → prioritized client experience → relevant content & actions → advisor interaction → updated client state → next relevant reason to return

# 4. Existing Concept Review & Key Observations
Area
Current understanding
Gap / observation
Discovery decision needed
Assessments & Forms
Assigned assessments are part of the client dashboard and advisor workflow; Risk Assessment is explicitly shown as an illustrative firm-controlled feature. Additional UX guidance states that assessment responses should influence the client experience rather than simply remain in a stored form or PDF.
The initial assessment inventory, questions, answer types, assignment rules, versioning/repeat frequency, and which responses affect personalization are not defined.
Confirm initial assessment set, content ownership, and the assessment-to-personalization mapping required for MVP.
Scoring / Results / Outputs
Assessments and results are fundamental to MVP; results are expected to contribute to planning status, opportunities and advisor review.
No scoring formula, weighting, category thresholds, output layout or historical comparison rules are defined in the overview.
Define scoring methodology, client-visible output and advisor-visible interpretation.
Planning Updates
A core recurring-engagement process: Due → Invitation → Client Completion → Changes Identified → Advisor Follow-Up → Dashboard Updated.
Form fields, cadence, due-date rules, change-detection rules, follow-up thresholds and client/advisor edit rights are not yet defined.
Define Planning Update business rules and workflow states.
Educational Content
Content should be surfaced because it is relevant, not only stored in a static library. Initial personalization may use age, life stage, client attributes, assessment responses, advisor assignments, client state/history and content tagging.
Content types, authoring ownership, taxonomy/tags, relevance rules, ranking, assignment/recommendation behavior, publishing lifecycle and firm-specific controls are not defined.
Define the MVP content model, tagging taxonomy, relevance rules and the simplest dynamic-content approach that demonstrates personalization.
Reminders & Engagement
Examples include Planning Update invitations/reminders, age/milestone reminders, educational communications and general planning reminders. Firm can use Automatic, Advisor Approval or Off.
Exact triggers, channels, timing, preferences, retry/failure behavior and template ownership are not defined.
Define minimum reminder/communication engine and approval model.
Benchmarking
Clients may compare selected measures such as retirement savings, income or net worth with age/cohort benchmarks; firm/advisor controls visibility.
Data source, cohort methodology, privacy thresholds, calculations, refresh frequency, explanatory caveats and supported measures are unresolved.
Confirm whether benchmarking is calculated from RetirementTrack data, external data, curated reference data, or a combination.
Client Experience
The dashboard should immediately help the client understand where they stand, what has been completed, what changed, what needs attention, what comes next and what is newly relevant. The UX guidance adds retirement-stage context, a prioritized set of attention items, dynamic content, progress continuity and a visible advisor connection.
Detailed first-login and return journeys, navigation, prioritization logic, life-stage definitions, personalization rules, empty states, minimum data requirements and client-level controls are not yet defined.
Validate the end-to-end personalized client journey and define the dashboard information/prioritization hierarchy for first login and subsequent returns.
Advisor Experience
Advisor dashboard should surface client activity, Planning Updates, changes, follow-up needs, tasks, alerts and communication history.
Client assignment model, advisor roles, task lifecycle, notification behavior and cross-client prioritization are not defined.
Validate advisor workflows and define the minimum actionable dashboard.
Firm / Tenant Controls
Firm control is central: feature toggles, client-level assignment/show-hide behavior, content, communications, branding and advisor identity.
Default settings, permission to change settings, inheritance/override behavior, tenant lifecycle and audit requirements are not defined.
Define configuration hierarchy and tenant-level governance.
Communication History
Basic history is expected to show invitation/reminder/completion/follow-up events.
Retention, event types, delivery status, advisor notes, filtering and export requirements are not defined.
Define MVP communication/audit history scope.
# Personalization & Prioritization
The preferred product model uses client data, life stage, assessments and circumstances as inputs to rules/personalization that produce a prioritized experience, relevant content/actions and advisor interaction.
The MVP rule model, life-stage categories, priority selection, state/history, missing-data behavior and advisor/firm override controls are not defined.
Define a simple rules-based MVP personalization model and the minimum architectural capabilities required to support it without introducing sophisticated AI.

# 9. Initial Risks / Concerns
ID
Risk / concern
Why it matters
Initial Discovery response
R-01
MVP scope expansion
The current vision contains many attractive capabilities; collectively they can become a large first release.
Prioritize the core engagement loop and explicitly simplify/defer secondary depth during Discovery.
R-02
Assessment/scoring ambiguity
Assessment questions, scoring, categories and outputs are not yet defined.
Identify content/methodology owner and define the minimum assessment model before technical design is finalized.
R-03
Planning Update complexity
This core workflow could expand into configurable forms, workflow automation and task management.
Define a narrow MVP state model, fixed initial form/rules and specific follow-up behavior.
R-04
Benchmark methodology
Benchmarking can be differentiated value but depends on credible source data, cohort definitions and privacy/caveats.
Validate source/methodology early and consider a curated/reference-data approach if live cohort analytics is not MVP-ready.
R-05
Communication complexity
Automatic/approval/off plus multiple triggers/channels/templates can become a notification platform.
Start with limited channels/triggers and a clear approval model; preserve extensibility.
R-06
Privacy / client data
Personalization, assessments and financial benchmarks may require sensitive client data.
Use data minimization; identify required fields, retention, access, logging and deletion rules during Discovery.
R-07
Tenant configuration complexity
Firm and client-level toggles can create inheritance/override and support complexity.
Define a small configuration hierarchy with clear defaults and auditability.
R-08
Advisor dashboard overload
Trying to surface every alert/task/client metric can make the first advisor experience noisy.
Prioritize actionable exceptions and follow-up states over broad analytics.
R-09
White-label scope growth
Custom domains, deep themes and branded messaging can materially increase implementation/testing.
Confirm a minimal white-label package for MVP.
R-10
Future integration pressure
CRM/email/calendar/planning-system integrations may distract from validating the core product.
Design stable integration seams, but defer integrations unless required to operate the MVP.
# R-11
Personalization scope expansion
Personalization can quickly expand into recommendation engines, AI, segmentation and complex ranking logic.
Constrain MVP to explicit, understandable rules and a limited set of inputs/outputs that demonstrate the client-specific experience.
# R-12
Rules / prioritization complexity
Independent rules across assessments, content, opportunities and reminders could become inconsistent and difficult to maintain.
Define one coherent MVP relevance/prioritization model with clear precedence and ownership before implementation.
# R-13
Insufficient client context
A personalized first-login experience may be weak when the platform has limited client data.
Define minimum onboarding/profile inputs, sensible empty/default states and progressive personalization as more information becomes available.
# R-14
Advisor trust and control
System-generated priorities or content may conflict with an advisor's judgment or firm preferences.
Define advisor/firm visibility, suppression/override and approval controls for the MVP where needed.
