Task 11 - Final Documentation, Review & Handoff
RetirementTrack MVP Discovery
# 1. Discovery Summary
RetirementTrack is a white-label B2B SaaS platform for retirement-planning and advisory firms. The MVP is designed to create an ongoing retirement relationship rather than a one-time assessment or collection of forms. It combines client self-service, recurring planning activity, educational content, reminders, benchmarking, and advisor visibility while intentionally minimizing routine advisor workload.
Operating Model: RetirementTrack guides and engages -> the client learns and acts -> the platform records progress and follows up -> the advisor sees engagement and true exceptions -> professional help is brought in where it adds value.
Product Dimension
MVP Direction
Client experience
Self-directed, personalized and recurring. A client should quickly understand where they are, what deserves attention, and what to do next.
Advisor experience
High visibility with low routine workload. Routine completion and reminders are platform-managed; advisors focus on client requests and defined professional-attention conditions.
Retirement lifecycle
Before retirement: "Am I prepared?" During retirement: "Am I staying on track?" Retirement is a stage transition, not an endpoint.
Education
A growing library is a core engagement mechanism, available through direct browsing and contextual surfacing.
Progress
Show visible planning/engagement progress, completed areas, upcoming milestones and outstanding items without claiming comprehensive financial readiness.
MVP boundary
No account aggregation, trading, portfolio management, generalized form/workflow builders, broad analytics engine or individualized automated financial/investment/tax/legal advice.
# 2. Functional Requirements
The MVP must implement the following end-to-end functional capabilities as a coherent experience rather than isolated modules.
Capability
Implementation Requirement
Firm setup & branding
Create/activate a firm tenant, configure logo and selected brand colors, establish firm administrators/advisors, and apply supported feature/content/communication settings.
Invitation & activation
Invite clients/advisors, activate accounts through Clerk, complete MFA and required acknowledgements, handle expired/resend/failure states, and map identity to tenant membership.
Client onboarding
Capture minimum profile, retirement context, household relationship, authorized household sharing acknowledgement, advisor assignment and initial personalization inputs.
Household experience
Support spouses/partners as separate authenticated users linked to one household. Maintain separate identity and consent for each person; share household-level information by default only after household-sharing acknowledgement; support a bounded Individual/Private classification for selected information without field-by-field permission complexity. Present one Primary Advisor to the client while the underlying model supports additional advisor relationships.
Client dashboard
Show a small, visually clear set of personalized priorities, accomplishments, milestones, retirement-journey/progress indicators, relevant education, Planning Update/assessment availability, benchmark entry points, notifications and Request Advisor Follow-Up. The dashboard should evolve as the client completes activities and should not feel like a dense task-management system.
Retirement journey
Present what has been addressed, what needs attention, what is coming next and what is relevant now across pre-retirement and post-retirement stages.
Planning Update
Periodically offer a lightweight, versioned opportunity for the client to report whether anything meaningful has changed. A No Change response must be fast; a Yes response branches only into relevant sections. Support save/resume for branched updates, prior/current comparison, history, event-triggered offers and optional advisor request. Only defined material conditions or explicit client requests create advisor-attention items; do not make the workflow a mandatory annual questionnaire by default.
Assessments
Provide a RetirementTrack-maintained, versioned catalog of approved assessments. Firms choose which approved assessments are available; advisors may recommend/assign supported assessments where enabled. Each assessment may have its own scoring/result model, history, retake rules and material-threshold logic. Do not create a single overall financial-readiness score and do not build a firm-authored assessment builder in MVP.
Education library
Provide a RetirementTrack-curated Learning Library containing approved videos, guides, one-pagers, infographics, articles/insights, factoids and checklists. Firms may select which platform-approved items/categories are available. Support browse/search/filter, contextual surfacing through controlled metadata/rules and meaningful engagement tracking. Do not build a full firm content-management system in MVP; preserve clean extension points for firm-specific content later.
Personalization
Use deterministic, explainable rules based on age, retirement stage, milestones, assessments, Planning Update changes, household context, completion history, enabled content/features and advisor recommendations. Rules should produce a bounded set of useful priorities/opportunities and contextual content, not a continuously expanding task list or AI recommendation engine.
Milestones & reminders
Use durable Temporal workflows for age/date milestones, retirement-stage considerations, Planning Update/assessment prompts and selected planning opportunities. Keep sequences bounded, avoid duplicate/noisy reminders, stop or suppress reminders when the item is completed/acknowledged/dismissed as allowed, and reserve advisor work for client requests or true exceptions.
Communications
Support email plus in-app notifications for MVP. Use Automatic / Advisor Approval / Off control patterns only where applicable, maintain intent/template/delivery history, and keep routine communication platform-led. Provide a simple Request Advisor Follow-Up action; do not include direct calendar integration in MVP.
Advisor visibility
Provide searchable assigned-client visibility separated into Engagement & Accomplishments, Outstanding Client Items, and Advisor Attention / Exceptions.
Advisor exceptions
Create advisor work only when a defined material condition, client request or approved rule warrants professional attention. Support acknowledge/review/resolve lifecycle.
Benchmarking
Use validated external/reference datasets only after source, methodology, cohort logic and usage rights are approved. The exact initial benchmark measures are not predetermined. Version the source/methodology, preserve historical reproducibility, present neutral caveats and an unavailable/not-calculable state when inputs or data are insufficient. Proprietary cross-client/cross-firm benchmarking remains outside MVP.
Firm administration
Manage supported firm users, invitations, advisor/client assignments, branding, enabled assessments/content, communication modes and bounded tenant configuration.
Platform administration
Manage tenants, approved assessment versions, platform content, benchmark datasets/versions, platform configuration and tightly controlled support operations.
Portability & offboarding
Support authorized export and a default transition window when a firm exits, followed by controlled deletion subject to legal/regulatory/backup/required exceptions.
## 2.1 Core Client Workflow
Invitation -> Account Activation -> MFA -> Consent / Household Sharing Acknowledgement -> Minimum Profile -> Household / Advisor Context -> First Personalized Dashboard -> Planning Update / Assessment / Education -> Progress & Milestones -> Reminders -> Advisor Contact or Exception when needed -> Returning Dashboard with updated state.
## 2.2 Planning Update Workflow
Periodic offer / approved event trigger / optional advisor request -> client opens -> asks whether anything meaningful changed -> No Change: confirm quickly, record result, refresh engagement state and stop applicable reminder sequence -> Yes: branch only into relevant sections, show prior values where useful, allow save/resume, submit versioned responses -> compare prior/current -> apply explicit material-change rules -> update progress/priorities/content -> create AdvisorException only for a configured material condition or explicit client request -> preserve history/version.
## 2.3 Assessment Workflow
Approved assessment is enabled by the firm and becomes available / is recommended / assigned -> client starts -> save/resume -> submit -> deterministic scoring/result logic for that published assessment version -> client-friendly result with caveats and relevant education -> preserve original version/history -> optional prior/current comparison where meaningful -> AdvisorException only for explicitly configured material result/threshold change or client request -> retake only according to that assessment cadence, advisor request, or appropriate material client change.
## 2.4 Advisor Workflow
Advisor signs in -> sees assigned-client summaries -> reviews Engagement & Accomplishments / Outstanding Client Items / Advisor Attention separately -> searches/filters households -> opens authorized client detail -> reviews relevant Planning Update/assessment changes -> acknowledges or resolves true exceptions -> responds to client-requested contact -> does not receive routine completion as a task.
## 2.5 Post-Retirement Engagement Workflow
Age/calendar/retirement-stage/client-context trigger -> evaluate approved deterministic rule -> surface education, priority, reminder or review prompt -> optionally encourage advisor contact -> create AdvisorException only if the approved rule warrants professional attention. Examples include RMD preparation/completion, withdrawal/draw-rate review, Medicare/IRMAA considerations, QCD opportunities, beneficiary/estate/insurance reviews, and income/spending changes.
## 2.6 Detailed Module Implementation Specifications
The following module specifications consolidate the final Discovery direction and latest product decisions into implementation-ready behavior. They define what another delivery team should build while preserving the approved Azure/.NET architecture and MVP boundaries.
### 2.6.1 Assessments
Assessments are a RetirementTrack-owned engagement capability. The MVP must support a defined, versioned catalog maintained by the platform; each firm controls which approved assessments are available to its clients. The catalog should remain extensible without introducing a generalized assessment/form builder.
Implementation Area
Required MVP Behavior / Developer Direction
Ownership & availability
Platform Admin manages approved AssessmentDefinition/AssessmentVersion records. Firm Admin enables/disables approved assessments for that tenant. An enabled assessment may be surfaced by deterministic eligibility rules or recommended/assigned by an authorized advisor.
Scoring model
Each assessment owns its own scoring/output contract: numeric score, category/band, flags, educational result or no-score completion. Persist the exact scoring/version reference with the result. Do not aggregate assessment outputs into one RetirementTrack financial-readiness score.
Lifecycle & history
Support available/recommended/assigned, started/in-progress, submitted, scored/completed and optional reviewed context as appropriate. Longer assessments support save/resume. Every completion remains tied to the exact published version used at that time; do not silently recalculate historical results after a version change.
Repeat / retake rules
Retakes are allowed only according to the cadence defined for that assessment, by authorized advisor request, or after an appropriate material client change. Avoid unrestricted repeated submissions.
Advisor impact
Normal completion is an Engagement/Accomplishment. Incomplete/due items remain client-facing/outstanding where applicable. Create AdvisorException only for assessment-specific configured material results/threshold changes or an explicit client request for help.
API / service responsibility
Expose tenant-authorized APIs for firm-enabled catalog, start/resume, response save, submit, result/history and advisor-authorized context. Scoring and threshold evaluation execute server-side against immutable/versioned definitions; UI must not be the source of scoring or authorization truth.
Failure / fallback
If scoring fails after submission, preserve submitted responses and submission version, show a processing/retry state, retry safely where appropriate, and alert operations if persistent. Never discard the client submission or fabricate a result.
Acceptance criteria
A firm can enable an approved assessment; an authorized client can complete it; the same inputs produce the deterministic versioned result; history remains reproducible after a newer version is published; cross-tenant access is blocked; configured material thresholds create an advisor exception and normal completions do not.
Out of scope
Firm-authored assessment builder, arbitrary rule scripting, unrestricted retakes, cross-assessment readiness engine, AI scoring/recommendations and automated individualized financial/investment advice.
### 2.6.2 Planning Updates
Planning Updates are a recurring engagement mechanism for detecting meaningful client changes with minimal friction. They are not a mandatory annual questionnaire by default and should not routinely depend on an advisor starting the process.
Implementation Area
Required MVP Behavior / Developer Direction
Trigger model
The platform periodically offers a change-check based on approved cadence/rules; appropriate life-stage/context events may also prompt an update. An advisor may request an update when useful, but advisor initiation is optional rather than the normal mechanism.
Fast No Change path
The first interaction should ask whether anything meaningful has changed. If the client selects No Change, record the confirmation/version/source/time, complete that offered interaction quickly, update engagement/progress state and stop any reminders associated with that offer.
Yes Change branching
If the client reports a change, route only to relevant sections/topics instead of forcing the entire questionnaire. Show prior known values where useful, permit safe save/resume for branched sections, validate server-side and persist the submitted version.
Comparison & materiality
Compare approved fields/topics to the relevant prior completed state and create explicit PlanningUpdateDetectedChange records. Separate informational/relevant changes from configured material conditions. A change may update personalization/priorities/content without creating advisor work.
Advisor impact
Completion/no-change is visible as engagement. Create AdvisorException only when an explicitly configured material rule is met or the client asks for advisor help. Do not create routine advisor tasks merely because an update was offered or completed.
API / service responsibility
Provide APIs for current offer/status, prior-value context, section responses, save/resume, submit/no-change confirmation, history and authorized advisor summary. Business services own trigger source, comparison, versioning, materiality and exception creation; Temporal owns durable timing/reminder behavior.
Failure / fallback
If comparison/materiality evaluation fails after a valid submission, preserve the completed update, mark evaluation pending/retryable, retry durably and avoid duplicate exceptions. The client should not need to resubmit because a downstream evaluation failed.
Acceptance criteria
No Change can be completed with minimal steps; Yes branches only to relevant sections; history/version is preserved; prior/current comparison is reproducible; reminders stop after completion; ordinary changes update the client experience without advisor work; only configured material conditions or client requests create exceptions.
Out of scope
Mandatory full annual questionnaire by default, generic workflow/form designer, advisor-required initiation, arbitrary scripting and automatic individualized financial/tax/legal recommendations.
### 2.6.3 Client & Household Structure
The MVP should support a household-oriented retirement experience while preserving individual identity, consent and bounded privacy. Keep the model simple enough for implementation and user comprehension.
Implementation Area
Required MVP Behavior / Developer Direction
Identity model
Each spouse/partner is a separate authenticated User with their own identity, contact information, acknowledgements and activity attribution. Household membership links authorized individuals without merging identities.
Minimum profile
Collect only information necessary to establish the first personalized experience and approved workflows, such as name, DOB/age, retirement status/timing, household relationship, advisor context and limited high-level planning information. Collect additional financial context only when a specific approved workflow requires it.
Sharing model
After explicit household-sharing acknowledgement, household-level planning information is generally shared among authorized household participants. Support a bounded Shared vs Individual/Private classification for selected information. Do not implement field-by-field spouse permission matrices in MVP.
Advisor relationship
Present one Primary Advisor in the client experience. The underlying AdvisorClientAssignment model may support additional advisor relationships/types and effective dates so the architecture does not require redesign later.
Authorization boundary
Every client/household API must verify authenticated tenant membership plus household membership or active advisor assignment. Tenant role alone is insufficient for access. Individual/Private data must be excluded from household responses and advisor views unless the requesting user is explicitly authorized.
Data minimization
Do not collect account credentials, account numbers, transactions, individual holdings or statement-vault data. High-level account type/ownership, approximate/current balance, income, savings or net-worth context may be stored only when needed by an approved workflow/benchmark.
Acceptance criteria
Two spouses can have separate logins linked to one household; shared information is available only after acknowledgement; Individual/Private information does not leak through household/client/advisor APIs; one Primary Advisor is shown; an advisor without an active assignment cannot access the household.
Out of scope
Complex per-field spouse permissions, unrelated-person household structures unless later approved, account aggregation, brokerage credential storage and unrestricted Firm Admin access to sensitive client information.
### 2.6.4 Educational Content
Education is a core recurring-engagement mechanism. RetirementTrack curates the master library; firms select which approved content is available. The MVP should support direct discovery plus deterministic contextual surfacing without becoming a full content-management product.
Implementation Area
Required MVP Behavior / Developer Direction
Content inventory
Support approved videos, guides/brochures, one-pagers, infographics, articles/brief insights, factoids/Did You Know items, checklists and benchmark explanations. Store type, title/summary, publish/availability metadata and controlled content references/assets.
Taxonomy
Use controlled tags for topic, life stage, age/milestone, workflow relevance and content type. Keep ContentItem-to-ContentTag many-to-many. Avoid uncontrolled free-form tagging that would make personalization inconsistent.
Firm availability
Platform Admin owns the master RetirementTrack library. Firm Admin may enable/disable approved content/categories for its tenant. MVP does not require firms to author/upload/manage a separate content library.
Contextual surfacing
Use deterministic rules based on age, retirement stage, milestones, Planning Update changes, assessment results, household context, prior completion and firm availability. Surface a bounded number of relevant resources; do not use AI ranking/recommendation in MVP.
Engagement tracking
Track meaningful states only: surfaced where useful, opened/viewed, downloaded, content-specific completed state and selectively dismissed/not interested. Do not collect exhaustive clickstream behavior. Advisor views should show meaningful engagement summaries rather than every page interaction.
Completion semantics
Use content-specific completion rules: e.g., watched threshold for a video when configured, explicit completion for checklist, opened/viewed for article/guide unless richer tracking is justified. Historical engagement should remain understandable if content is later unpublished.
Failure / fallback
If content is unpublished/removed, do not break historical engagement records; show unavailable/replacement behavior where appropriate. If a contextual rule references unavailable content, omit it and continue with other eligible items rather than failing the dashboard.
Acceptance criteria
Clients can browse/search/filter enabled content; firms can select availability from the platform catalog; contextual rules never surface disabled content; meaningful engagement is persisted; historical activity remains readable after content changes; no cross-tenant exposure occurs.
Out of scope
Full firm CMS, arbitrary firm-authored content workflows, theme marketplace, AI recommendation engine, marketing automation and exhaustive behavioral tracking.
### 2.6.5 Benchmarking
Benchmarking is an optional educational comparison capability using validated external/reference data. The exact measures are a product decision and must not be hard-coded around income, net worth or retirement savings simply because those were earlier examples.
Implementation Area
Required MVP Behavior / Developer Direction
Definition model
Implement BenchmarkDefinition, BenchmarkDataSource, BenchmarkDataVersion, optional Cohort/reference values, ClientBenchmarkResult and visibility/availability metadata. The model must support different future measures without schema redesign.
Activation criteria
Do not activate a benchmark until the source, methodology, cohort rules, effective period, legal/usage rights and client-facing explanation/caveat are approved. Exact initial benchmark measures remain TBD by product/domain review.
Calculation
Use deterministic server-side calculation against the active validated data version and only the minimum client/household inputs required for that measure. Persist enough source/version/method references to reproduce the historical result.
Presentation
Show a neutral comparison with source/year or effective period, methodology/caveat and educational explanation. Avoid judgmental good/bad labels and avoid implying financial readiness or professional advice.
History & updates
Preserve historical results with the dataset/methodology that applied when generated. A newly validated data version may generate a new current comparison; do not silently rewrite old results.
Unavailable state
If required client inputs, a validated source/version or cohort value are missing, return unavailable/not-calculable with a clear reason rather than fabricating or extrapolating a result.
Tenant / privacy boundary
Firm enablement and client/advisor visibility remain tenant-scoped. Do not query or expose identifiable data from other RetirementTrack firms/clients to build MVP comparisons.
Acceptance criteria
A product-approved benchmark can be loaded/versioned/activated; calculation is reproducible; historical results survive source updates; invalid/inactive source versions cannot produce new results; missing inputs show unavailable; presentation includes provenance/caveat.
Out of scope
Proprietary cross-client/cross-firm benchmarking, live identifiable peer comparisons, unvalidated scraped data, hard-coded specific initial measures and broad analytics/reporting engine.
### 2.6.6 Client Priorities
Client priorities should guide attention without turning RetirementTrack into a task manager. The dashboard should show a small number of useful items drawn from milestones, opportunities, current engagement context and explicit client/advisor needs.
Implementation Area
Required MVP Behavior / Developer Direction
Priority sources
Allow priority candidates from client-requested/advisor-attention conditions, time-sensitive milestones, offered/current Planning Updates or assessments, relevant planning opportunities, recommended education and freshness/discovery content.
Selection behavior
Use deterministic, explainable business rules with explicit priority/urgency/date and source/reason metadata. Apply a bounded display limit so lower-value items do not crowd the experience. Exact weighting/tie-break values remain configurable/product-approved rather than embedded permanently in UI code.
Consumer framing
Use language such as relevant now, coming up, worth reviewing, learn or discuss where appropriate. Reserve required/due wording for items explicitly configured as such. Milestones and opportunities should coexist with actionable items.
State changes
Re-evaluate priorities after meaningful client activity, Planning Update changes, assessment outcomes, milestone changes, content completion/dismissal and advisor/client actions. The dashboard should visibly evolve after progress.
Traceability
Every surfaced priority should carry enough internal metadata to explain which rule/source produced it and why. Do not expose sensitive underlying values on broad screens when a concise reason is sufficient.
Acceptance criteria
The same client state/rule version yields the same bounded priority set; completing/dismissing an eligible item updates subsequent results; disabled content/features cannot generate priorities; conflicting rules follow explicit precedence/configuration and are traceable.
Out of scope
Infinite task feed, AI-generated priorities, opaque ranking, generalized rules builder and advisor task creation for every client interaction.
### 2.6.7 Reminders & Milestones
Reminders should support useful recurring engagement, not create recurring chores. Temporal Cloud should provide durable timers/retries while business rules control what is worth surfacing and when.
Implementation Area
Required MVP Behavior / Developer Direction
Trigger inventory
Support approved age/date milestones, retirement-stage events, offered Planning Updates, enabled assessment prompts, selected post-retirement considerations and other product-approved planning opportunities. Do not assume every possible retirement topic is a mandatory workflow.
Workflow execution
Temporal workflows/activities should carry TenantId plus stable business identifiers, load authoritative data at execution time, schedule due/next times, retry idempotently and support cancellation/stop conditions. Avoid carrying unnecessary sensitive payloads inside workflow state.
Bounded sequences
Where repeated reminders are appropriate, use a bounded sequence with frequency caps. Prevent duplicate active reminders for the same source and avoid too many simultaneous client prompts.
Stop / dismiss behavior
Completion/acknowledgement stops applicable routine reminders. Informational/educational items may be snoozed or dismissed when allowed. Planning Update prompts follow the lightweight offer model rather than being universally mandatory; any hard-required state must be explicitly configured.
Advisor impact
Routine reminder delivery remains platform-led. Advisors should not receive tasks for normal reminder activity. Create advisor work only for a configured professional-attention condition or client-requested follow-up.
Failure / fallback
Delivery/workflow failures use bounded retries and record operational status. Idempotency must prevent duplicate communication or duplicate advisor exceptions. Persistent failures should be observable through monitoring without losing the underlying client state.
Acceptance criteria
Timers survive worker restarts; completion cancels remaining reminder steps; duplicate triggers do not create duplicate active reminders; tenant context is preserved; informational snooze/dismiss works where configured; routine reminders do not create advisor tasks.
Out of scope
Broad marketing automation, high-frequency campaign sequencing, mandatory completion of every reminder, unmanaged cron jobs and advisor-created custom workflow designer.
### 2.6.8 Communications & Advisor Follow-Up
MVP communication channels are email plus in-app notifications. Communications should be traceable, provider-abstracted and mostly platform-led, with advisor approval only where a supported scenario specifically requires it.
Implementation Area
Required MVP Behavior / Developer Direction
Channels
Implement email and in-app notification delivery for MVP. Use Twilio SendGrid as the production transactional email provider behind an application abstraction so domain logic stores intent/template/status identifiers rather than coupling business rules directly to SendGrid.
Control modes
Where applicable, support Automatic, Advisor Approval and Off. Routine operational reminders generally use Automatic; selected sensitive/advisor-specific scenarios may require Advisor Approval; firms may disable supported categories where product policy allows.
History
Persist intent/source workflow, template/version, approval state, SendGrid message/provider reference where applicable, delivery status, recipient/sender identity references, related entity and timestamps. Avoid storing/logging unnecessary sensitive message payloads.
Request Advisor Follow-Up
Provide a simple client action to request advisor contact/follow-up. Create a trackable client-initiated request/exception visible to the assigned advisor, with acknowledge/resolve state as appropriate. The action should not require calendar integration.
Sender identity
Use a RetirementTrack-controlled Twilio SendGrid account and authenticated sending domain. Resolve RetirementTrack/approved firm branding and sender configuration through tenant/application settings. Sender-domain behavior remains controlled configuration rather than hard-coded logic.
Failure / fallback
On SendGrid delivery failure, bounce, block or deferred event, use bounded retry where appropriate, record delivery state and surface operational alerts when significant. Process SendGrid event/webhook callbacks idempotently and do not duplicate sends on retries. In-app state remains independently available when email delivery fails where the workflow supports it.
Acceptance criteria
Routine eligible communications send automatically once through Twilio SendGrid; approval-required items do not send before approval; Off suppresses the supported communication; SendGrid delivery/approval history is auditable; client follow-up requests reach the correct assigned advisor; cross-tenant recipients cannot be selected.
Out of scope
Direct calendar scheduling/integration, SMS/push for MVP, marketing campaign automation, arbitrary free-form advisor blasts and provider-specific business logic embedded in domain services.
### 2.6.9 Client Dashboard, Retirement Journey & Progress
The client dashboard is the primary engagement surface, not a generic collection of widgets. It must feel like an engaging consumer product and visibly evolve as the client completes activities, reaches milestones, reports changes and engages with education.
Implementation Area
Required MVP Behavior / Developer Direction
Dashboard composition
Provide first-login and returning-client states with a bounded set of relevant-now priorities, retirement-journey/progress visualization, age/retirement timeline, milestones, recent accomplishments, Planning Update and assessment availability/status, relevant education, benchmark entry points where enabled, notifications and Request Advisor Follow-Up.
Journey behavior
Show what has been addressed, what remains relevant, what is approaching and where attention may be useful. Use completed assessments, Planning Updates, milestones, checklists/content engagement and outstanding-item state as inputs. Do not present the dashboard as a comprehensive financial plan or task backlog.
State evolution
Recompute dashboard state after meaningful client actions, detected Planning Update changes, assessment results, milestone changes, content completion/dismissal and advisor/client follow-up actions. Completion must visibly affect accomplishments, progress and what is surfaced next.
Consumer framing
Use plain language such as relevant now, coming up, worth reviewing, learn and discuss. Reserve required/due language for explicitly configured core items. Avoid dense financial-services tables as the default client experience.
API / service responsibility
Expose one client-dashboard aggregation endpoint/use case that composes authorized profile/household context, progress state, priority candidates, milestones, enabled content, workflow status and advisor contact information. Do not make the React client independently reproduce business prioritization logic.
Security / privacy
Return only data authorized for that client/household. Individual/Private information must not leak through household-level cards, progress summaries, search, notifications or advisor-facing summaries.
Failure / fallback
If one optional source such as benchmark/content data is unavailable, render the rest of the dashboard and show a bounded unavailable state instead of failing the page. Missing profile data should produce a clear prompt only when needed.
Acceptance criteria
First-login and returning states differ appropriately; completing a supported activity visibly updates the dashboard; only a small bounded priority set is displayed; disabled content/features do not appear; household/private boundaries are preserved; the dashboard remains useful before and during retirement.
Out of scope
Comprehensive financial-planning dashboard, portfolio/account aggregation, unlimited task feed, AI-generated advice, trading/account performance views and generalized dashboard-builder functionality.
### 2.6.10 Advisor Dashboard & Exception Management
The advisor experience should maximize visibility while minimizing routine work. Normal client engagement, incomplete client items and true professional-attention conditions must remain structurally separate.
Implementation Area
Required MVP Behavior / Developer Direction
Assigned-client scope
Show only clients/households for which the advisor has an active authorized assignment in the current tenant. Advisor role alone must not grant firm-wide client access.
Primary views
Provide separate Engagement & Accomplishments, Outstanding Client Items and Advisor Attention / Exceptions views. Client completion belongs in engagement; due/incomplete routine activity belongs in outstanding items; only material conditions or client requests belong in advisor attention.
Search / filters
Support bounded server-side search/filtering by client/household, status, exception reason/priority, recent activity and advisor assignment where appropriate. Lists should be paginated and tenant-scoped.
Client drill-down
Allow authorized drill-down to relevant profile/household summary, progress, Planning Update changes, assessment result/history context, milestones, content engagement and exception history. Broad list views should avoid unnecessary sensitive values.
Exception lifecycle
AdvisorException supports source, reason, severity/priority, created time, status, acknowledgement/review/resolution, concise notes and related client/workflow identifiers. Material/high-risk conditions remain visible until resolved or appropriately closed.
Advisor follow-up
Client Request Advisor Follow-Up creates a clearly identifiable client-initiated item. Advisor can acknowledge and resolve it; no scheduling/calendar integration is required for MVP.
Failure / fallback
If an underlying client item becomes unavailable or authorization changes, preserve audit/history while preventing stale unauthorized drill-down. Duplicate rule evaluation must not create duplicate active exceptions.
Acceptance criteria
Normal client completions never automatically become advisor tasks; defined material rules and client follow-up requests do; unauthorized clients cannot be opened by URL/API manipulation; exception transitions are auditable; advisor summaries remain usable without exposing excess sensitive data.
Out of scope
Advisor CRM replacement, broad case-management platform, generalized task/workflow builder, calendar scheduling integration and automatic individualized financial/investment/tax/legal recommendations.
### 2.6.11 Firm Administration & White-Label Configuration
Firm Admin should have enough control to operate a tenant without turning RetirementTrack into a fully configurable platform. Administration is tenant-scoped and should not automatically expose sensitive client planning data.
Implementation Area
Required MVP Behavior / Developer Direction
User administration
Manage supported firm users, invitations, advisor/client assignments, activation/deactivation and practical role combinations such as Advisor + Firm Admin where explicitly allowed.
Branding
Configure firm logo, selected brand colors, firm display identity and the client-visible Primary Advisor presentation. Branding must use bounded validated settings rather than arbitrary theming/CSS.
Assessment / content availability
Enable or disable approved RetirementTrack assessments and approved platform content/categories for the tenant. Firm Admin selects from platform-maintained catalogs; it does not create assessments or operate a full content-management system.
Communication settings
Manage supported Automatic / Advisor Approval / Off modes and approved sender/firm identity settings where applicable. Firm settings cannot bypass global safety/security boundaries.
Sensitive-data boundary
Firm Admin is an operational role, not an implicit permission to read all assessment responses, financial-planning data or Individual/Private household information. Any broader business visibility requires an explicit authorized role/policy.
Auditability
Role changes, assignments, invitation actions, branding/configuration changes and enable/disable decisions must create appropriate AuditEvents with tenant, actor, timestamp and target identifiers.
Acceptance criteria
Firm Admin can operate users/assignments/branding/catalog availability within one tenant; cannot manage another tenant; cannot create unsupported assessments/content/workflows; sensitive client detail is not broadly exposed solely because of Firm Admin status.
Out of scope
Full CMS, form builder, workflow designer, custom rules scripting, arbitrary theme editor, broad reporting engine and unrestricted support-style access to client data.
### 2.6.12 Platform Administration
Platform Admin controls RetirementTrack-owned master catalogs and tenant lifecycle. This is a privileged operational capability outside normal tenant membership and must be tightly controlled and audited.
Implementation Area
Required MVP Behavior / Developer Direction
Tenant lifecycle
Create/activate/suspend supported firm tenants, manage bounded platform configuration and support controlled operational transitions without granting default client-data browsing.
Master catalogs
Manage approved AssessmentDefinition/Version records, platform-curated content, validated benchmark data/source versions and other centrally controlled configuration required by the MVP.
Publishing / versioning
Published assessment/content/benchmark versions that support historical results must remain reproducible. Platform changes should use explicit active/effective states rather than silently modifying historical definitions.
Privileged operations
Require named identities, MFA, least privilege, explicit authorization and AuditEvents for platform operations. Break-glass/support access is exceptional and purpose-limited.
Failure / safety
Prevent activation of invalid benchmark versions or incomplete assessment definitions; use confirmation/guardrail patterns for destructive or cross-tenant-impacting operations; preserve audit history.
Acceptance criteria
Platform Admin can manage master catalogs and tenant lifecycle without being a default super-user for client data; privileged actions are auditable; published historical behavior remains reproducible; invalid/inactive versions cannot be used for new production results.
Out of scope
Unrestricted production database browsing, tenant impersonation as a normal workflow, generalized no-code platform builder and bulk cross-tenant analytics on identifiable client data.
### 2.6.13 Identity, Invitations & Onboarding
Clerk provides managed authentication, but RetirementTrack owns tenant membership, application roles, advisor/client relationships and business authorization. Onboarding should collect only enough information to create the first useful personalized experience.
Implementation Area
Required MVP Behavior / Developer Direction
Invitation lifecycle
Support secure invite creation, intended tenant/role context, expiry, resend/revoke, already-used/invalid invite states and activation completion for client, advisor and supported admin roles.
Identity mapping
Map Clerk external user identity to internal User/UserExternalIdentity and TenantUserMembership records. Clerk Organizations are not the authoritative tenant model.
MFA
Require production MFA for clients and privileged users using supported Clerk capabilities; enforce privileged-route checks server-side and provide recoverable enrollment/account-recovery behavior.
Client onboarding
Collect minimum profile and retirement context, household relationship, household-sharing acknowledgement, advisor context/Primary Advisor and initial personalization inputs. Additional information should be gathered later only when a supported workflow requires it.
Authorization after activation
Every authenticated request resolves active tenant membership and user identity server-side. Browser-supplied TenantId/role values are never accepted as proof of authorization.
Failure / fallback
Handle expired invitations, duplicate identity, disabled account, failed activation, missing membership, incomplete MFA and stale session states with clear user-facing recovery paths and auditable operational diagnostics.
Acceptance criteria
A new invited user activates into the intended tenant/role, completes required MFA/acknowledgements and reaches the correct onboarding/dashboard path; invalid/expired invites fail safely; duplicate identities do not create duplicate tenant users; cross-tenant identity manipulation is blocked.
Out of scope
Custom-built authentication provider, password storage by RetirementTrack, automatic trust of identity-provider organization membership and collection of unnecessary financial information during initial signup.
### 2.6.14 RetirementTrack Progress / Preparedness Meter
A simple visible progress indicator in the MVP, but it must measure coverage/completion of the client's applicable retirement-planning journey - not whether the client has enough money to retire.
Implementation Area
Required MVP Behavior / Developer Direction
Meaning
Represent the portion of applicable planning/engagement areas that have been addressed, together with accomplishments, remaining areas and approaching milestones. The label and explanatory text must avoid implying comprehensive financial readiness.
Inputs
Use existing versioned states such as completed assessments, Planning Updates, applicable milestones/checklists, selected educational activities and configured engagement/outstanding states. Only inputs explicitly designated as progress-relevant should contribute.
Applicability
Determine which journey areas apply based on retirement stage, age/milestones, household/profile context and enabled product configuration. A non-applicable area must not reduce the client's progress.
Calculation
Use a bounded deterministic calculation with versioned configuration/weights if weighting is needed. The same applicable-state snapshot and rule version must produce the same result. Preserve enough metadata to explain contributing areas.
Presentation
Show a simple percentage/progress indicator plus addressed/remaining/approaching areas. Before retirement it supports the question Am I prepared?; during retirement it supports Am I staying on track? without answering either as a financial adequacy conclusion.
Advisor visibility
Advisor may see the same journey coverage and underlying areas, but progress alone must not create an AdvisorException. Exceptions continue to come only from explicitly configured material rules/client requests.
Acceptance criteria
Progress changes when designated applicable areas are completed; non-applicable areas are excluded; historical or current state can be explained; no screen/text claims RetirementTrack has determined financial readiness; no generalized scoring engine is introduced.
Out of scope
Retirement income sufficiency calculation, Monte Carlo analysis, portfolio success probability, individualized retirement advice and a universal readiness score across assessments/financial data.
### 2.6.15 Personalization & Rule Evaluation
Personalization should make the experience feel relevant while remaining deterministic, explainable and testable. It is a controlled product-rule layer, not an AI recommendation engine or generalized rules platform.
Implementation Area
Required MVP Behavior / Developer Direction
Inputs
Use approved client/household profile fields, age/date milestones, retirement stage, enabled assessments/results, Planning Update changes, milestone state, content engagement/completion, outstanding items, firm configuration and explicit advisor recommendations.
Outputs
Generate bounded priority candidates, relevant content, milestone/reminder prompts, workflow availability and context labels. Professional-attention output is allowed only when an explicitly approved exception rule is met.
Rule model
Store controlled rule definitions/version references and a constrained application vocabulary. Avoid arbitrary scripts/expression execution. Each surfaced output should carry source/reason/rule-version metadata.
Precedence / conflicts
Use explicit precedence/tie-break handling when multiple rules compete. Avoid contradictory simultaneous priorities and suppress duplicates. Exact product weighting can remain configuration, but behavior must be deterministic.
Tenant / availability
Rules must respect firm-enabled features/content and tenant boundaries. Disabled content/assessment or unavailable data cannot generate a user-facing recommendation.
Failure / fallback
If a rule references missing/inactive data, omit or degrade that output safely and record a diagnostic reason. A rule failure must not prevent unrelated dashboard content from loading.
Acceptance criteria
Given the same authorized client state and rule versions, the same outputs are generated; each output is explainable; conflicts resolve consistently; disabled resources never surface; no opaque AI-generated advice is produced.
Out of scope
LLM/AI recommendation engine, generalized no-code rule builder, arbitrary tenant-authored scripts, hidden behavioral scoring and automated individualized financial/investment/tax/legal advice.
### 2.6.16 Audit, Activity History & Explainability
RetirementTrack needs two distinct histories: business/client engagement history for product experience and AuditEvent records for security, administration and accountability. Diagnostic telemetry is a third separate concern.
Implementation Area
Required MVP Behavior / Developer Direction
Engagement history
Record meaningful accomplishments and workflow state changes such as completed Planning Updates/assessments, milestone acknowledgements, meaningful content completion and benchmark reviews where product value exists.
Audit events
Record significant administrative/security/lifecycle actions such as role/assignment changes, privileged access, configuration changes, tenant lifecycle, export/deletion requests, exception transitions and release-sensitive operations where applicable.
Explainability
For personalized outputs, assessment results, benchmark comparisons and advisor exceptions, retain source identifiers, version references and reason metadata sufficient to explain why the system produced the outcome.
Sensitive data
Do not duplicate raw assessment responses, detailed financial fields, credentials, tokens or sensitive payloads into diagnostic logs. Audit/engagement records should use safe identifiers and concise reason/status metadata.
Retention / access
Access to audit history is role/purpose limited. Final retention periods are a production policy decision, but the model should support retention/deletion requirements without relying on immutable unrestricted logs.
Acceptance criteria
A support/security investigation can identify who changed a key configuration and when; a developer/advisor can trace the source/version of a material system outcome; logs do not contain prohibited sensitive payloads; tenant boundaries apply to all histories.
Out of scope
Full event-sourcing architecture, surveillance-style clickstream analytics and storing every UI interaction as an audit event.
### 2.6.17 Data Lifecycle, Export & Firm Offboarding
The MVP must preserve RetirementTrack ownership/control and provide a bounded path for authorized export and firm exit without enabling cross-firm data exposure or uncontrolled retention.
Implementation Area
Required MVP Behavior / Developer Direction
Export
Support authorized tenant/client data export in a documented format appropriate to the approved MVP data set. Export authorization must be explicit and tenant-scoped; export generation/download should be audited and protected.
Offboarding window
Use the established default 30-day transition window after firm termination for authorized export/portability unless a later business/legal decision overrides it.
Deletion orchestration
After the transition window, begin controlled deletion/deactivation subject to legal/regulatory/audit/backup/required-retention exceptions. Track request, approval/authority, status, timestamps and completion evidence.
Cross-firm boundary
Do not automatically transfer a client/household to another firm or expose data across firms. Any future portability/transfer workflow requires explicit authorization and separate product/legal rules.
Backups / historical systems
Deletion semantics must account for managed backup lifecycle and documented exceptions rather than promising immediate removal from every backup copy. Restore procedures must not inadvertently re-enable deleted access.
Acceptance criteria
Authorized export is tenant-scoped and auditable; offboarded users lose active access appropriately; deletion lifecycle can be tracked; cross-tenant data is never included; documented retention exceptions are respected.
Out of scope
Automatic cross-firm client migration, indefinite retention by default, data-broker style reuse and undocumented manual database exports.

# 6. Multi-Tenancy
For MVP, one Firm equals one Tenant conceptually. The system should not introduce office/branch/business-unit hierarchy until a concrete product requirement exists. Tenant ownership is explicit on every tenant-owned record and is enforced at multiple layers.
Control Layer
Required Enforcement
Authenticated tenant context
Resolve TenantId/UserId server-side from active membership/identity. Do not accept client-provided TenantId as proof.
EF Core read isolation
Apply centrally defined global query filters for tenant-owned entities. Normal services must not use IgnoreQueryFilters().
Write enforcement
SaveChanges interception/service validation sets/verifies TenantId, blocks cross-tenant references and prevents TenantId mutation.
Authorization policies
Combine tenant + role + relationship/assignment checks for advisor/client operations.
Database constraints
Use TenantId-aware unique/composite indexes and foreign-key patterns so cross-tenant relationships cannot be created accidentally.
Selected SQL RLS
Use SQL Server Row-Level Security with session context for additional defense in depth where practical.
Raw SQL
Restricted, code-reviewed and required to explicitly preserve tenant scope.
Background workflows
Every Temporal workflow/activity carries TenantId plus stable business identifiers and re-establishes tenant context before data access.
Automated testing
Cross-tenant negative tests are mandatory for APIs, administrative operations and background workflows.

## 9.1 API Design Standards
Use versioned, explicit DTOs/contracts rather than exposing EF entities.
Validate authorization before returning or mutating tenant-owned data.
Use idempotency for operations that may be retried by workflows or external callbacks.
Return stable error codes/messages suitable for UI behavior without leaking sensitive system details.

Keep large files outside API/database payloads; store references to controlled Blob assets.

# 16. Confirmed Decisions & Final Implementation Guardrails
The following decisions should be treated as the current product source of truth for implementation. They are not reasons to reopen the approved architecture; they clarify how the MVP should behave and where the first-build boundary sits.
Confirmed Decision
Implementation Direction
Assessment catalog
RetirementTrack develops and maintains a defined/versioned assessment catalog. Firms choose which approved assessments are available. Do not hard-limit the catalog to exactly 2–3 and do not build a firm-authored assessment builder.
Assessment scoring
Individual assessments may have their own score/category/flags. Do not create one overall score that claims RetirementTrack has determined whether a person is financially ready to retire.
Visible progress
Show client progress, addressed areas, accomplishments, remaining areas and approaching milestones. A bounded RetirementTrack Progress/Preparedness Meter may measure applicable journey coverage, not financial adequacy.
Planning Updates
Use periodic lightweight change-check opportunities rather than a mandatory annual questionnaire. No Change should be fast; Yes branches only into relevant sections. Event prompts are allowed; advisor requests are optional.
Advisor attention
Only explicitly defined material conditions or client-requested follow-up create advisor-attention work. Routine completion, reminders and ordinary outstanding client items remain platform-managed.
Client / household
Use a relatively simple initial profile, separate spouse/partner identities and consent, shared household information after acknowledgement, bounded Individual/Private classification and no complex field-by-field spouse permissions.
Advisor relationship
Present one Primary Advisor to the client while the underlying model supports additional advisor relationships/types for future growth.
Priorities
Keep priority rules flexible and consumer-oriented. The experience should mix milestones, opportunities, education and relevant planning considerations rather than becoming an always-on required-task list.
Reminders / communications
Email plus in-app is sufficient for MVP. Twilio SendGrid is the selected transactional email provider. Routine communication is platform-led; support bounded reminders and Automatic / Advisor Approval / Off modes where appropriate. Informational items can be snoozed/dismissed when configured. Keep SendGrid behind a provider abstraction so the product is not tightly coupled to one vendor.
Advisor follow-up
Provide a simple Request Advisor Follow-Up capability. Do not include direct calendar/scheduling integration in MVP.
Benchmarking
Use only validated external/reference data. Do not preselect income, net worth or retirement savings as mandatory initial benchmarks. Activate only comparisons with clear retirement-planning value, validated methodology/source and approved usage rights. Proprietary cross-client/cross-firm benchmarking remains outside MVP.
Content
RetirementTrack curates the core educational library; firms select which approved content is available. No full firm content-management system is required in MVP, but the architecture should support future firm-specific content without major redesign.
Consumer UX
The client experience should feel like an engaging consumer platform, using visual progress, age/retirement timelines, milestones, accomplishments and priority/opportunity cards that visibly evolve with activity.
High-fidelity design first
Complete high-fidelity UI/UX designs/prototypes for the principal Client, Advisor, Firm Admin and Platform Admin experiences before significant frontend development.
Architecture
The overall Azure/.NET/React/Clerk/Temporal modular-monolith direction is accepted and does not need to be reopened because of these product refinements.
Independent validation
Include an independent pre-production implementation-level security/architecture review by someone separate from the implementation team, covering tenant isolation, access controls, Azure configuration, secrets, logging, backup/recovery, release controls and other material controls.
Operations still to finalize
Specific business-approved RPO/RTO, production support expectations and detailed retention periods may be finalized before production; implementation should preserve the documented operational direction and extension points.
Commercial MVP objective
Completion means more than a technical/internal pilot: after approved UI/UX, implementation, QA/UAT, production hardening, independent review/remediation and launch readiness, the MVP should be polished and secure enough to demonstrate and sell to outside advisory firms, while still excluding later enterprise capabilities such as broad integrations, advanced reporting and deep customization.
